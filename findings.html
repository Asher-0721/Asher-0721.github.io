CROWDFUNDING MEDICAL

Subsidizing the cost of medical expenses

Illnesses

Dementia And Ageing

The Phase 1 planning document used information from Alzheimer's South Africa (ADASA), which estimated that about 187,000 people in South Africa were living with dementia, with the number expected to rise to around 250,000 by 2030. These figures are estimates recorded in the planning document and should be treated as such, rather than as a newly verified count. They show why planning for care and support is important as the population ages.

Research Reference

Cancer Treatment Costs

The Phase 1 summary highlighted that certain advanced cancer treatments can cost hundreds of thousands of rand, with an example course of treatment potentially costing nearly R1 million (Discovery, n.d.). This is a good example of how quickly treatment costs can become too expensive for an individual's household to afford. The amount of money required will vary depending on the type of disease, the course of treatment, and the patient's personal circumstances.

Research Reference

Parkinson's Disease

The table in Phase 1 provided a link to a database of South African research on Parkinson's disease published on PubMed. In addition, it highlighted the growing burden of Parkinson's disease in sub-Saharan Africa and the lack of local data on its prevalence. Better data can help healthcare facilities identify patients' needs. Meanwhile, crowdfunding can help individuals cover certain medical expenses that their families cannot afford.

Research Reference

Why The Problem Persists

The Phase 1 summary identified several reasons for medical funding shortfalls. Public healthcare services may involve delays, private treatment can be expensive, and medical aid may not cover all expenses. Crowdfunding cannot replace healthcare services or guarantee that treatment will be affordable, but it may help bridge the financial gap by covering specific expenses, such as medication, transport, or caregiving.

MAIN TRUST FACTORS

11 out of 15 respondents (73.3%) said they were likely or very likely to donate. The most selected donation range was R101–R250, chosen by 8 respondents (53.3%). These are stated intentions, not evidence that respondents donated money.

WILLINGNESS TO DONATE

Respondents selected medical proof, platform reputation and regular updates as the leading trust factors, with four respondents choosing each. Three respondents selected a clear explanation of spending. This supports the Phase 1 finding that campaigns should explain the need, use a reputable platform, show suitable evidence and keep donors informed.
